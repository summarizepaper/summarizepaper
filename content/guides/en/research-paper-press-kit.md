Prepare to explain a paper in public while keeping its evidence intact. This example starts from a generated press kit, then checks which wording can survive outside the scientific article.

## A real example

The kit uses 1703.10693v1, a model of gas in debris discs. Its brief states that only the abstract was read. The “I am asked to comment” view then provides strengths, weaknesses and open questions, with passages found in that source. The three roles offer different material: choose the one that fits your task.

## Reproduce the workflow

1. Open the Press kit for 1703.10693v1 with Premium+ and check the source and verification status.

2. Choose the material for your role: presenting your own paper, commenting as a researcher, or reporting on the study. Read the findings and supporting passages before choosing a headline.

3. Check numbers, qualifications and quotations. Prepare a question for the authors where the source does not resolve an uncertainty, then export the material you need.

## Turn the result into a decision

Keep a model prediction distinct from an observation, and a sentence from a paper distinct from an interview quotation. Suggested outside commentators need independent checks of expertise and conflicts. A useful kit gives you evidence to work with; it does not replace editorial judgement.
