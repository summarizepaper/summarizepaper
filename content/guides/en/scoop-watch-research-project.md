You plan to study the origin of gas in debris discs. A new paper asks a similar question through dust dynamics. An older paper models how primordial gas survives. Which is useful background, and which part of your project needs a sharper explanation?

**Scoop Watch**, available with Premium, compares papers with your project's question, method, data and expected results. This walkthrough follows an actual **28 October 2026** demonstration: an initial search, a more precise project description, a **CC BY full-text comparison** and a saved reading task. The project is fictional and contains no private data.

## Describe a contribution, not just a topic

Open [Scoop Watch](/en/scoops/). Give the project a title and at least 300 characters explaining what it will do. Our initial description combined a gas-evolution model with spatially resolved observations to distinguish gas-production mechanisms. It named no specific targets or new findings.

| --- | --- |
| Question | Which competing explanations will you distinguish? |
| Method | What will you model, measure or compare? |
| Data | Which systems, observations or datasets will you use? |
| Expected result | What would this work add, without presenting a plan as a finding? |

You can also start from a draft PDF. The option to retain its text concerns later full-text comparisons. This demonstration used a written description only.

## Set references, teams and a manageable alert frequency

The options let you exclude your own work through coauthor names, follow particular authors and supply key references. New papers by followed authors are read even if their similarity is low; papers citing several key references can also enter the comparison.

The screenshot shows an **unsaved example**: Wataru Ooyama as a followed author, `2608.22781` as a known reference, and immediate emails for strong overlaps with partial overlaps grouped weekly. These option trials did not change the existing project's email settings.

## Separate new announcements from prior work

“New papers” follows announcements. “Already published” searches the previous three years across arXiv, journals and OpenReview. The first check and prior-work results appear on the page; they are not evidence of an email alert received.

The new-paper list includes *Unbound grains in gaseous debris discs*. Its **partial overlap** concerns the shared gas-origin question, while its dust-halo diagnostic differs from the initially proposed gas-distribution comparison.

The first assessment uses **abstracts**. “All”, “Strong overlaps” and “Partial overlaps” filter the displayed matches. A search result count measures neither completeness nor the novelty of your project.

## Refine the project when the first description is too broad

A vague description cannot establish whether papers use your intended data or method. We edited the demonstration to specify **one-dimensional gas evolution, thermochemical calculations, CO and neutral-carbon radial profiles, HCO+ strengths, and matched beam and sensitivity treatment**. It still makes no claim of new measurements or established findings.

The new pass reported 58 close papers and added further comparisons. Earlier matches remain in the project; their original briefs are not automatically rewritten to reflect every edit. The numbers from successive passes should not be added as a count of unique studies.

## A partial overlap can still be essential reading

The refined search returned [A Primordial Origin for the Gas-rich Debris Disks around Intermediate-mass Stars](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02195), by **Nakatani et al.**, published in **The Astrophysical Journal Letters in 2023**, DOI **10.3847/2041-8213/ad0ed8**, under **CC BY 4.0**.

Read “In common” and “What stays yours” together. The latter suggests distinctions to verify; it does not certify originality. A partial overlap can deserve a citation and affect how you frame the project.

## Read beyond the abstract before deciding what is new

Choose **“Compare with the full text”**. For this openly licensed paper, the completed comparison gives a question–method–data–results table and a more specific assessment. If full-text analysis is unavailable, use the abstract-level result with that limitation in mind.

## Save the paper with the question it changes

We marked the Nakatani paper as a **“Real overlap”** in the demonstration project. This records relevance without changing the partial-overlap label. “Not relevant” reduces the weight of similar papers; “Undo” removes your feedback.
