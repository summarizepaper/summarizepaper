See where publication activity is changing, then read the papers behind the signal. Rising topics gives a dated reason to investigate a subject rather than a prediction of its future importance.

## A real example

We inspected the astrophysics view for the submission week beginning 12 October 2026. This is a field-specific Premium result. Keep the selected week with the screenshot; it is not evidence that the same topics are rising in every later week.

## Reproduce the workflow

1. Open the overview or a Premium field view and note the submission week. Our example uses Astrophysics and the week beginning 12 October 2026.

2. Inspect the curve and supporting papers. The method compares weekly term shares with the preceding eight weeks and requires evidence from several teams.

3. Read two papers and decide whether they share a scientific question or merely vocabulary. Save a collection or a query if the connection matters to your work.

## Turn the result into a decision

A rise can follow a data release, instrument or conference deadline. It does not establish that the findings are correct or that growth will continue. Use the history to distinguish a sudden increase from a recurring pattern; use Weekly letters when you want the results explained as a narrative.
