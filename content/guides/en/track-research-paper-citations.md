A citation count does not explain how a paper is being used. Start with a familiar reference, then inspect the citing sentences behind the new connections.

## A real example

We opened the followed paper 1703.10693 and launched its citation-context analysis. The observed record contained 135 citations, with a citing sentence available for only some of them. The French analysis eventually classified 60 citations. This is an on-demand analysis of existing citations, not proof of a newly received email alert. Inspect the sentence behind a label before interpreting it.

## Reproduce the workflow

1. Open Citation alerts with Premium and follow a paper by arXiv identifier, DOI or link. The example uses 1703.10693.

2. Open its citation detail page and request context analysis where available. Keep self-citations separate when this matters.

3. Read a citing sentence and its surrounding paragraph before accepting a label such as “uses it” or “questions it”. Choose the email frequency that fits your routine.

## Turn the result into a decision

Save important follow-up papers to a collection. A missing citation sentence limits what can be inferred; indexing delays also mean that an empty history does not establish that nobody has cited the paper. Scoop Watch can discover close work that never cites the reference.
