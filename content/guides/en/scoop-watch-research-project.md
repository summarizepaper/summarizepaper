A new paper can overlap your project without using your keywords. Scoop Watch compares the question, method, data and expected contribution; our example uses an explicitly fictional project.

## A real example

This fictional project compares gas-production scenarios with resolved observations of debris discs. Its first check of new announcements found no close papers. The separate historical search failed, including after a retry, so this example demonstrates project setup and the announcement check. A three-year search for the French version of the project returned 67 close papers; its results are illustrated in the [French guide](/fr/guides/scoop-watch-research-project/). A completed announcement check is distinct from a historical search and does not demonstrate delivery of an email alert.

## Reproduce the workflow

1. Describe the question, method, data and expected contribution of your project. Our fictional case compares gas-production scenarios with resolved observations of debris discs.

2. Review the first announcement check separately from the three-year prior-art search. Add key references or author exclusions only when they make sense for your project.

3. For a match, read what is shared and what remains different, then open the paper before deciding to cite it or clarify your project’s scope.

## Turn the result into a decision

A match is a reading lead, not proof of priority or a verdict that a project has been scooped. Keep the description current as the project changes. Saved searches complements this semantic watch when you also need a specific expression tracked.
