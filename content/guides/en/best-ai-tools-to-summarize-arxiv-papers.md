Key takeaways

- **To understand one paper fast**, use a tool that works from its identifier: [SummarizePaper](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/) (a full summary page for arXiv, journal and preprint papers, with answers you can check passage by passage) or [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/) (discussion and chat on top of arXiv). Neither needs an upload.
- **For questions across papers you already chose**, NotebookLM and the SummarizePaper assistant both answer from the full text and show their sources.
- **For a systematic review over hundreds of papers**, Elicit and SciSpace. For an exploratory review from a research question, SummarizePaper Premium+.
- **No AI summary replaces the paper.** Prefer tools that show where each claim comes from, then check the claims that matter.

## Which tool should you pick?

The right tool depends on the job, not on a ranking:

| Your job | Pick |
|---|---|
| Decide in a minute whether a paper is worth reading | SummarizePaper, alphaXiv |
| Check where each claim of an AI answer comes from in the paper | SummarizePaper, NotebookLM |
| Discuss a paper with other readers | alphaXiv |
| Ask questions across a dozen papers you already chose | NotebookLM, SummarizePaper chat |
| Run a systematic review over hundreds of papers | Elicit, SciSpace |
| Get a first map of the literature on a research question | SummarizePaper Premium+, Elicit |
| Check your draft's bibliography and find papers it misses | SummarizePaper Premium |
| Turn papers into flashcards and structured notes | Scholarcy |
| Summarize a PDF that has no DOI or arXiv ID | ChatPDF, SciSummary, ChatGPT or Claude |
| Follow new papers in your field every morning | SummarizePaper daily digest |

## The tools, one by one

### SummarizePaper

[SummarizePaper](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/) started as a summarizer for arXiv and is now a research companion for papers from arXiv, journals (from their DOI), bioRxiv, medRxiv, HAL, Europe PMC and OSF preprint servers. Three things set it apart.

**Answers you can check.** Each paper page has a detailed summary, key points, a lay summary, a blog-style article, related papers across sources and the BibTeX entry, as [this example](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/paper/2302.13303v1/) shows. Newer pages add a structured reading (question, method, findings marked as result or interpretation, limitations) where each passage shown is word for word in the paper. The [AI assistant](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/chat/) answers from the full text of one or several papers, reads their figures, and after each answer lists its claims with the sentence of the paper that supports each one, the section or figure it comes from and a link that opens the paper on that passage. Claims the paper does not support are labelled as such instead of hidden. The full text is read only when the paper's license allows it; otherwise the page says the summary comes from the abstract.

**Readers of every level.** Every summary is written in English or French and can be rewritten in 16 languages and four reading levels, from a curious 12-year-old to an expert.

**Tools for the work between papers.** Beyond reading:

- *Follow your field*: a free [daily digest](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/digest/) of new arXiv, bioRxiv and medRxiv papers, weekly letters, rising topics and saved searches with email alerts; with Premium, citation alerts that say what citing papers write about yours, alerts when a paper gets a new version, and a scoop watch that emails you when a new paper comes close to your project.
- *Write and submit* (Premium): [What did I miss?](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/missed/) finds the close papers your draft does not cite; the [bibliography check](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/refcheck/) flags retracted papers, preprints published since, and citations the cited abstract does not support.
- *Review a question* (Premium+): the [literature review](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/literature-review/) turns a research question into searches on OpenAlex and arXiv, follows the references of the closest papers to find the founding ones, and writes a synthesis of 15 to 30 papers grouped by approach, showing every search and count so you can judge the coverage.
- *Present and meet* (Premium+): journal club slides you can edit and export to PowerPoint, press kits with source-checked quotes and a hype check, a conference companion that picks the talks that matter for you, and research placement leads for students.
- Collections with comparison tables and open questions, and exports to Zotero, Notion and Obsidian.

- **Strengths:** works from an identifier for arXiv and many journals and preprint servers; answers checked against the paper's own sentences; the widest set of research tools in this list, in English and French.
- **Limits:** you cannot upload a PDF to summarize it (only a draft or a conference program, in the research tools); no community discussion; no flashcards; the literature review is exploratory, not a systematic review. Built and run by one person.
- **Price:** free within [daily limits](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/faq/) (10 new summaries and 100 questions a day with a free account). [Premium](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/premium/) is €7 per month for the most capable model, no summary limit, 500 questions a day and the research tools; Premium+ is €14 per month and adds the literature review, journal club, press kits, the conference companion and research placements.

### alphaXiv

[alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/) adds a layer on top of arXiv: replace `arxiv` with `alphaxiv` in a paper's URL to open it there. Readers can comment on papers, and alphaXiv adds AI chat on the paper and AI-written overviews in several languages.

- **Strengths:** the discussion is the unique part; it works straight from any arXiv URL.
- **Limits:** arXiv only; the overview is a starting point rather than a structured summary with lay version and BibTeX.
- **Price:** no paid plan listed on its site in October 2026.

In detail: [alphaXiv vs SummarizePaper](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/guides/alphaxiv-vs-summarizepaper/).

### NotebookLM

Google's [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google/) answers questions only from the sources you add to a notebook, and cites them. The free plan allows 50 sources per notebook and 100 notebooks; paid Google plans raise these limits.

- **Strengths:** answers grounded in your own selection of documents; audio overviews.
- **Limits:** you collect and upload the papers yourself; nothing is specific to research papers.

### Elicit

[Elicit](https://elicit.com/) is built for literature reviews: search across more than 138 million papers, then extract findings into tables. Its free Basic plan includes unlimited summaries and chat with papers; the Pro plan is $49 per month and adds systematic review workflows.

- **Strengths:** the best fit for structured, systematic evidence work over many papers.
- **Limits:** more than you need to triage one paper.

### SciSpace

[SciSpace](https://scispace.com/) combines literature search, chat with PDFs and explanations of highlighted passages while you read. It suits students reading papers outside their field. Check [its pricing page](https://scispace.com/pricing) for current plans.

### Scholarcy

[Scholarcy](https://www.scholarcy.com/) turns articles into flashcard-style summaries with background, methods and findings. Its free summarizer is limited to 10 summaries. The monthly plan, €8.99 per month after a one-week trial, adds unlimited summaries, enhanced summaries, flashcard collections, a literature matrix to compare papers and one-click bibliographies.

Looking elsewhere? See [Scholarcy alternatives](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/guides/scholarcy-alternatives/).

### SciSummary

[SciSummary](https://scisummary.com/) summarizes papers you upload and analyzes their figures. It offers a 7-day free trial, then a Pro plan at $48 per year.

### ChatGPT and Claude

General assistants summarize any PDF you upload and answer follow-up questions well. You write the prompt yourself, free plans are limited, you have to ask explicitly for the passages behind an answer, and the result stays in your chat instead of a page you can come back to or share.

### Semantic Scholar

[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/) shows a one-sentence TLDR for many papers in its search results. Useful to scan a list quickly; too short to understand a paper.

## Who SummarizePaper is not for

Pick another tool if you need to summarize PDFs that have no DOI, arXiv ID or other public record (see [ChatPDF vs SummarizePaper](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/guides/chatpdf-vs-summarizepaper/)), if you run a systematic review over hundreds of papers, or if you want a discussion with other readers. SummarizePaper is built for researchers who read, follow and cite papers every week, and want to check what an AI tells them about each one.

## How to check an AI summary in two minutes

1. Read the abstract and check the summary says the same thing.
2. Check one number from the summary against the paper's results section. On SummarizePaper, the passage behind each claim of the assistant is one click away.
3. Look at the limitations or caveats the authors state; summaries tend to drop them.
