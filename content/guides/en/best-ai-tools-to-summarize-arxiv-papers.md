Key takeaways

- **To triage one arXiv paper fast**, use a tool that works from the arXiv ID: [SummarizePaper](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/) (a full summary page, in English or French) or [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/) (discussion and chat on top of the paper). Both work without uploading anything.
- **For questions across your own set of papers**, NotebookLM answers only from the sources you add, with citations.
- **For a systematic literature review**, Elicit and SciSpace search and extract across millions of papers; they are overkill for one paper.
- **No AI summary replaces the paper.** Use summaries to decide what to read, then check claims at the source.

</div>

## Which tool should you pick?

The right tool depends on the job, not on a ranking:

| Your job | Pick |
|---|---|
| Decide in a minute whether an arXiv paper is worth reading | SummarizePaper, alphaXiv |
| Discuss a paper with other readers | alphaXiv |
| Ask questions across a dozen papers you already chose | NotebookLM, SummarizePaper chat |
| Run a literature review over hundreds of papers | Elicit, SciSpace |
| Turn papers into flashcards and structured notes | Scholarcy |
| Summarize any PDF, not only arXiv | SciSummary, Scholarcy, ChatGPT or Claude |
| Get new papers in your arXiv categories every morning | SummarizePaper daily digest |

## The tools, one by one

### SummarizePaper

[SummarizePaper](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/) publishes AI summaries of arXiv papers, generated from the full text: a detailed summary, the key points, a lay summary anyone can understand, a blog-style article, related papers and the BibTeX entry. Type an arXiv ID or search on the home page; if the paper was already summarized, the page shows up immediately, as [this example](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/arxiv-id/2302.13303v1/) does. An [AI assistant](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/chat/) answers questions from the full text of one or several papers, and a free [daily digest](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/digest/) emails new papers in your arXiv categories with two-sentence summaries.

- **Strengths:** free to read with no account, summaries are public pages you can share and cite, English and French, digest built for following arXiv.
- **Limits:** arXiv papers only, no upload of your own PDFs. Starting a new summary takes a few minutes the first time. Built and run by one person.
- **Price:** free within [daily limits](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/faq/); [Premium](https://www.summarizepaper.com/en/premium/) is €7 per month for the most capable model, no summary limit and 500 questions a day.

### alphaXiv

[alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/) adds a layer on top of arXiv: replace `arxiv` with `alphaxiv` in a paper's URL to open it there. Readers can comment on papers, and alphaXiv adds AI chat on the paper and AI-written overviews in several languages.

- **Strengths:** the discussion is the unique part; it works straight from any arXiv URL.
- **Limits:** the overview is a starting point rather than a structured summary with lay version and BibTeX.
- **Price:** no paid plan listed on its site in October 2026.

### NotebookLM

Google's [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google/) answers questions only from the sources you add to a notebook, and cites them. The free plan allows 50 sources per notebook and 100 notebooks; paid Google plans raise these limits.

- **Strengths:** answers grounded in your own selection of papers; audio overviews.
- **Limits:** you collect and upload the papers yourself; nothing is arXiv-specific.

### Elicit

[Elicit](https://elicit.com/) is built for literature reviews: search across more than 138 million papers, then extract findings into tables. Its free Basic plan includes unlimited summaries and chat with papers; the Pro plan is $49 per month and adds systematic review workflows.

- **Strengths:** the best fit for structured, multi-paper evidence work.
- **Limits:** more than you need to triage one paper.

### SciSpace

[SciSpace](https://scispace.com/) combines literature search, chat with PDFs and explanations of highlighted passages while you read. It suits students reading papers outside their field. Check [its pricing page](https://scispace.com/pricing) for current plans.

### Scholarcy

[Scholarcy](https://www.scholarcy.com/) turns articles into flashcard-style summaries with background, methods and findings. Its free summarizer is limited to 10 summaries; the paid plan offers unlimited summaries with a one-week trial.

### SciSummary

[SciSummary](https://scisummary.com/) summarizes papers you upload and analyzes their figures. It offers a 7-day free trial, then a Pro plan at $48 per year.

### ChatGPT and Claude

General assistants summarize any PDF you upload and answer follow-up questions well. You write the prompt yourself, free plans are limited, and the result stays in your chat instead of a page you can come back to or share.

### Semantic Scholar

[Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/) shows a one-sentence TLDR for many papers in its search results. Useful to scan a list quickly; too short to understand a paper.

## Who SummarizePaper is not for

Pick another tool if you need to summarize PDFs that are not on arXiv, if you run a systematic review over hundreds of papers, or if you want a discussion with other readers. SummarizePaper is built for one job: understanding arXiv papers quickly, in English or French, and following new ones in your field.

## How to check an AI summary in two minutes

1. Read the abstract on arxiv.org and check the summary says the same thing.
2. Check one number from the summary against the paper's results section.
3. Look at the limitations or caveats the authors state; summaries tend to drop them.
