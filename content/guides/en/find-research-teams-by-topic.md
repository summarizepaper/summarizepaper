Map the approaches to a research question before comparing names. This example explores gas production and evolution in debris discs through recent publications.

## A real example

The French run retained 24 papers from 293 search results and grouped them into two approaches. A separate English run retained 22 papers from 334 results and three approaches. These dated samples vary with the generated queries; they are not a census or a stable ranking.

## Reproduce the workflow

1. Describe the topic in Who works on it? and choose an overview, collaborators, competing teams or possible reviewers.

2. Choose a two- or five-year publication window. Inspect the generated queries and adjust them if the selected papers are too broad.

3. Compare the approaches tab with representative papers. Then inspect people and institutions, including the publication roles and identifiers behind a name.

## Turn the result into a decision

Use the map to identify a complementary method or a paper to read. Author order is not a universal measure of leadership. Check current affiliations; for reviewer suggestions, the ORCID-based exclusions help but do not replace the journal’s conflict checks.
