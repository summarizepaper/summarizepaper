{% trans "Frequently Asked Questions" %}

{% trans "Papers and summaries" %}

{% trans "What is this website about?" %}
{% blocktrans %}SummarizePaper helps you read, follow and use research with AI. For any paper from arXiv, a journal or a preprint server, you get a detailed summary, the key points, a lay summary and a blog-style article, which you can have rewritten in 16 languages and for four reading levels. An AI assistant answers your questions about one or several papers with the sentences of the papers that support its answers. Collections keep your papers together and compare them; a daily digest and alerts follow your field; and tools for researchers help you check a bibliography, review a question, prepare a journal club or a conference.{% endblocktrans %}
{% trans "Which papers can I summarize?" %}

{% trans "Every arXiv paper, and papers from many other sources:" %}

  • {% trans "Journal articles: any paper with a DOI. Paste the DOI or the link of the article, or search the open-access literature from the home page (we use OpenAlex, which lists more than 250 million works)." %}
  • {% trans "bioRxiv and medRxiv preprints." %}
  • {% trans "HAL, ChemRxiv, the OSF preprint servers (PsyArXiv, SocArXiv, EarthArXiv, EdArXiv...), and the articles of PubMed and Europe PMC: paste their link, their PMID or their PMCID." %}

{% trans "When a journal article also has an arXiv version and its own license does not let us read it, we take you to the arXiv page. You cannot upload your own PDF to have it summarized." %}

{% trans "How do I get the summary of a paper?" %}
{% trans "Search for a paper with the search bar on the home page (on arXiv, or in the journals and preprints), or paste its arXiv ID, its DOI or its link. If the paper has already been summarized, the summary shows up immediately. If it is the first time someone looks for it, our AI has to read and summarize it first, which can take a few minutes; the progress is shown on the page. You can open the PDF of the paper beside its summary to compare them." %}
{% trans "Why is a summary sometimes made from the abstract only?" %}
{% trans "A summary is a derivative work, so our AI reads the full text of a paper only when its license allows it: CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA or CC0 (public domain). For the other papers (arXiv's default license, “no derivatives” licenses, articles behind a paywall), the summary and the assistant use the title, the authors and the abstract, and the page says so. Many bioRxiv preprints, for instance, are under CC BY-NC-ND." %}
{% trans "How does the AI summarization work?" %}
{% trans "We read the full text of the paper and send it to recent OpenAI language models with a series of prompts that produce each part of the summary. For arXiv, we read the HTML version when it exists and the PDF otherwise; for journals and other preprint servers, the structured text of the publisher or of Europe PMC when there is one, an open PDF otherwise, or the arXiv version. Free users get our standard model; Premium subscribers get our most capable model, for summaries as well as for answers." %}
{% trans "Can I read a summary in another language or for another reader?" %}
{% blocktrans %}Yes. On the page of a paper, ask for a version in one of 16 languages (English, French, Spanish, German, Chinese, Japanese, Arabic, Hindi...) and for one of four readers: a 12-year-old, a high school student, an undergraduate or an expert. Each version is written once from the summary and the abstract, then shared by every reader. Versions already written are free to read; you can ask for {{ readings_free }} new ones per day without Premium, as many as you want with Premium.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "The AI assistant" %}

{% trans "Does the AI assistant read the whole paper?" %}
{% trans "Yes, when the license of the paper allows it (see above); otherwise it answers from the abstract and tells you so. When a paper is too long, the assistant leaves out its references and appendices first, then keeps the passages that matter most for your question, and each paper says how much of it is read. Its answers appear as they are written: you can stop, copy or ask again." %}
{% trans "How do I know that an answer is right?" %}
{% trans "Each claim of an answer comes with the sentence of the paper that supports it, and we check that this sentence is word for word in the paper. A claim whose sentence is not found, or a number no paper states, is marked. When several papers are discussed, tags such as [Smith et al. 2021] say which one each claim comes from and link to it. The assistant can still be wrong: read the passages before you rely on an answer." %}
{% trans "Can I question or compare several papers at once?" %}
{% blocktrans %}Yes, on the chat page: search arXiv, the journals and the preprint servers in one list, and add up to {{ chat_max_papers }} papers. You can reorder them, which orders the answers. With Premium, a comparison table reads each paper whole and gives each value with the passage of the paper it comes from.{% endblocktrans %}
{% trans "Are my conversations saved? Can I share them?" %}
{% blocktrans %}Your recent conversations are kept: you can come back to them, rename them and delete them, and export an answer with the BibTeX of its papers. A conversation can be shared as a public, read-only page of what it was when shared, which never says who asked. The link works for {{ share_days }} days after it was last shared, and you can stop sharing it at any time; deleting the conversation stops it too.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Collections" %}

{% trans "What is a collection?" %}
{% blocktrans with free_collections=collections_free.collections free_papers=collections_free.papers premium_collections=collections_premium.collections premium_papers=collections_premium.papers %}A collection keeps papers together for a project: add them from any paper page, or import a BibTeX or RIS file or a Zotero collection. Each paper can have notes, tags and a reading status, and shows its year, journal, editorial status, citations and newer version. A preprint and its published version count as one work. You can chat with a whole collection, order its papers and export them (BibTeX, RIS, Markdown or a spreadsheet) with your notes and tags. A free account has {{ free_collections }} collections of {{ free_papers }} papers, Premium {{ premium_collections }} collections of {{ premium_papers }} papers.{% endblocktrans %}
{% trans "What do the comparison tables and open questions of a collection give?" %}
{% trans "With Premium, a comparison table puts the papers of a collection side by side, in columns from a template (general, clinical, machine learning, observation, theory) or of your own. Each value keeps the passage of the paper that supports it, quoted word for word, and you can correct a value yourself. The analysis then lists where the papers agree, disagree and what they leave open, with the passages behind each point. It can also find what the collection may be missing: references several of its papers share and the closest papers it does not hold." %}
{% trans "Can I share a collection or work on it with colleagues?" %}
{% trans "Yes. A read-only link shows the collection to anyone, who can copy it into their own account. An invitation link lets colleagues edit it with you: they add, remove, annotate and order papers, and run the analyses on their own plan. Only you can rename, share, watch or delete it, and you can stop either link at any time." %}
{% trans "What does watching a collection do?" %}
{% trans "With Premium, a watched collection follows its papers: you get an email when they are cited, when one gets a new version, and when one is published in a journal, retracted or corrected." %}

{% trans "Following your field" %}

{% trans "How does the daily digest work?" %}

{% blocktrans %}On the digest page, choose up to {{ max_categories }} arXiv categories, bioRxiv and medRxiv subject areas or journal subfields, which cover every journal and other preprint servers such as ChemRxiv, OSF or SSRN (or import them from your ORCID iD or arXiv author identifier), optional keywords, the kind of summaries (for researchers: contribution, method and main result; or 2 sentences in plain language), their language and the time you want to receive it. While you edit, the page shows what you would have received from the latest announcement, and you can send yourself a preview.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Every weekday, you get up to {{ papers_per_email }} new papers, with a link to the full AI summary of each and to a web page where every paper of the day is ranked. No digest is sent on Saturday and Sunday in your time zone. You can also receive it once a week instead. Every email has a link to unsubscribe in one click.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans with categories=premium_digest.categories papers=premium_digest.papers digests=premium_digest.digests %}With Premium, the digest ranks papers by closeness to a description of your research or to reference papers (by arXiv ID or DOI), and can hold every paper close enough rather than a fixed number. It follows authors across all categories, adds a synthesis of the week in your fields, has the full summary of your 3 best papers ready before you open the email, and can be listened to as audio. It can also gather your scoop, citation, new version and publication alerts instead of separate emails. You can have up to {{ digests }} digests, with {{ categories }} categories and {{ papers }} papers each.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Which alerts can I receive?" %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Saved searches: an email when new papers match a search (one with a free account, five with Premium).{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Citation alerts (Premium): who cites your papers or the papers you follow and what they say about them, what changed when one gets a new version, and when one is published, retracted or corrected.{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Scoop watch (Premium): an email the day a new paper comes close to your project in progress.{% endblocktrans %}
  • {% blocktrans %}Weekly letters: an article per theme on the papers that matter, on the web for everyone and by email with Premium.{% endblocktrans %}
{% trans "Is the description of my project private in the scoop watch?" %}
{% trans "Yes. Only you see it. It is used to compare your project with the papers: an AI rewrites it as an English abstract and splits it into facets, and they are sent to our AI provider for that comparison only. If you give the PDF of a draft, it is read once to write the description and is not stored, unless you ask us to keep its text, stored encrypted, to compare it with the full text of close papers; you can delete that text at any time. You can delete a project at any time." %}

{% trans "Tools for researchers" %}

{% trans "Which tools do you offer researchers?" %}

{% trans "Besides the summaries, the assistant, collections and the digest:" %}

  • {% trans "To follow your field: citation alerts, the scoop watch, saved searches, weekly letters, the rising topics of arXiv and a map of the groups that work on a topic." %}
  • {% trans "To write and submit: a literature review from a research question, “What did I miss?” for the close papers your draft does not cite, a bibliography check, and exports to Zotero, Notion and Obsidian." %}
  • {% trans "To present and meet: journal club slides, press kits, a conference companion and research placement leads for students." %}

{% blocktrans %}See all the tools and what each offer includes.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "What does the literature review do?" %}
{% trans "From a research question, it finds 15 to 30 founding and recent papers, groups them by approach and writes a synthesis that cites them (Premium+). It is an exploratory review to start reading a question, not a systematic review: check the papers it cites and complete them with your own search." %}
{% trans "How does the bibliography check work?" %}
{% trans "Give your .bib file, and your LaTeX source if you want. We look for each paper on arXiv, OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar: retracted papers come from Retraction Watch through OpenAlex, and preprints published since get their corrected BibTeX entry from Crossref. With the LaTeX source, an AI reads each sentence that cites a paper next to the abstract of that paper. It only knows the abstract, so a flagged citation is something to check, not a proof of error. Your files are not stored." %}
{% trans "How do I make journal club slides?" %}
{% trans "Open the page of the paper and, in its tools, click “Slides to present it” (Premium+). In about a minute you get around ten slides with what to say on each, the strengths and limits of the paper and questions for the discussion. The figures of the paper are included when its license allows it (CC BY and similar). Present them full screen, or save them as PDF from your browser." %}

{% trans 'You can edit the slide text and speaker notes, choose the position and size of figures, and save a private version. The PDF uses a 16:9 slide format, with one slide per page and no speaker notes.' %}

{% trans 'Download the PowerPoint (.pptx) to continue editing text, moving figures and using speaker notes in PowerPoint.' %}

{% trans 'Choose French or English before generating the slides. The chosen language applies to the presentation and its exports, independently from the website language. Previous presentations and edits remain available.' %}

{% trans "Can I export a summary or a reference?" %}
{% trans "Yes. Each paper page lets you download the summary as a PDF, and export the paper to BibTeX, RIS (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) or Markdown (Notion, Obsidian), whether it comes from arXiv, a journal or a preprint server. With Premium, you can also send it to your Zotero library with its summary." %}
