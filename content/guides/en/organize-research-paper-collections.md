You are preparing a journal club on the first image of Sagittarius A&#42;. One paper gives the overall result, another explains image reconstruction, and a third tests gravity. How do you keep their contributions straight and quickly find what you need to explain?

Use **Collections** to build a working dossier: a note and reading priority for each paper, a selection for your talk, then a comparison with supporting passages. This guide follows a demonstration actually completed with three EHT publications.

## Choose papers with different roles

All three papers were **published in 2022** and deposited on arXiv in November 2023. We use the versions below, all under **CC BY 4.0** and available in full text. The year displayed on the cards is their journal publication year.

| Paper | Role in our preparation |
| --- | --- |
| [I — Overview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08680v2) | Introduce the image, measurements and overall interpretation. |
| [III — Imaging](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09479v1) | Understand image reconstruction and its uncertainties. |
| [VI — Metric tests](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09484v1) | Separate the observed ring from the inferred shadow and examine tests of gravity. |

This small corpus is a focused starting point. The papers analyze the same 2017 EHT campaign: they provide complementary contributions, rather than three independent observational confirmations.

## Create the collection and import the references together

Open [Collections](/en/collections/), sign in and create **“Journal club — Sagittarius A*: from image to gravity”**. Under **“Add papers”**, paste these identifiers, one per line, then click **“Add”**:

```text
2311.08680v2
2311.09479v1
2311.09484v1
```

## Give every paper a purpose

In **“Papers”**, enter notes, tags and reading statuses. Changes save automatically; wait for **“Saved”** before leaving the page.

We marked Paper I as a **“Key paper”**, with the tags `overview, talk`. Its note already makes a decision for the presentation:

> Open the talk with Figure 3: a robust ring, smaller images are not time frames. Do not present consistency with Kerr as proof of uniqueness.

Paper III is **“To read”**, tagged `imaging, talk`, to explain reconstruction families and variability treatment. Paper VI is **“To read”**, tagged `gravity, further-reading`, for the discussion of ring-to-shadow calibration.

**Filtering and selecting are separate actions.** A filter does not deselect hidden papers. To restrict the analyses as well, choose **“Only the tag ‘talk’”** in the selection menu and click **“Apply”**. Check the counter: **2 of 3 papers selected**. Checkboxes control which papers go to the chat, comparison and open questions; reading statuses organize your work.

Click **“Chat with the selected papers”**. In this demonstration, the chat opens with I and III only, both marked **“Full text”**.

## Build a table around your presentation

With **Premium**, open **“Comparison table”**, choose **“Only my columns”** and define your presentation criteria. Each line gives a label, then what to look for after the colon:

```text
Contribution of the paper: this paper’s role in interpreting the image of Sagittarius A*
Result for the talk: one central result, with the quantity and its uncertainty if given
Limitation to mention: one limitation explicitly stated by the authors, without inventing one
```

**Available full text does not mean every analysis reads every word.** The Collections table extracts key sections and also uses the abstract. Here, all three papers could be read beyond their abstracts; the displayed indicator confirms the scope. When the license does not permit that, the analysis uses the abstract and the available summary as context.

## Open the source before quoting a conclusion

In Paper VI’s row, expand **“Source”** under **“Limitation to mention”**. The original passage explains that a quantitative calibration is required to connect the bright-ring diameter to the corresponding shadow diameter.

“Nine of nine” means passages were found, not that every interpretation is automatically correct. Check that the passage supports the wording and quantity in the cell. **“Correct”** lets you edit a cell; the change is labeled as a manual correction.

Below the table, agreements, differences and gaps point back to the rows used. Something missing from this small grid may still be covered in the full paper: read it before concluding that the authors did not study it.

## Prepare the discussion questions

Under **“Open questions”**, run **“Find the open questions”**. The English analysis produced nine questions and three project ideas. They include the robustness of ring-to-shadow calibration and the challenge of recovering source dynamics with sparse observations.

For a journal club, this gives you a useful closing question: which observations or synthetic-data tests would reduce the uncertainty? Suggested projects are starting points to assess. **A question left open in these 2022 papers may have been addressed since**: check later research before turning it into a research proposal.

## Find a complementary reading, then take your notes with you

**“What is missing”** looks for references cited by several papers and for works close to the corpus. Here, the Ghez and colleagues study of stellar orbits appears as a reference cited by two collection papers.

Suggestions help extend your reading; they do not certify a complete bibliography. Similarity results can concern a different object or method, so open the paper and decide how it contributes to your question.

Use **Markdown** for working notes, **BibTeX** for LaTeX, **RIS** for a reference manager or **Spreadsheet (CSV)** for the annotated list. The comparison table has its own CSV export. A tag filter lets you export just the papers carrying that tag; collection exports preserve notes and tags.

Collections with notes, tags and reading statuses are available with a free account. The comparison table, open questions and suggestions shown here require **Premium**. **“Share and settings”** also provides read-only sharing and collaborative editing. The demonstration collections remained private, with no invitation or email watch enabled.
